\begin{center}
    \Large{\textbf{ABSTRACT}}\\
\end{center}
\vspace{1cm}
In educational digital transformation, the demand for artificial intelligence in teaching and learning is growing rapidly, especially for high school Computer Science. Students need a tool that can answer textbook-based questions and generate practice exercises, while teachers need support for preparing presentation slides and lesson plans. Existing solutions mainly follow two directions. General-purpose chatbots provide flexible responses, but they may generate incorrect or out-of-scope information. Traditional document retrieval systems are more source-grounded, but they are often less effective at producing natural and instructionally useful outputs. Therefore, the key problem is to build a system that remains faithful to textbook content while still being flexible enough to support both learning and teaching tasks.

To address this problem, the thesis adopts an approach that combines textbook retrieval with content generation based on the retrieved materials. This direction was chosen because it can reduce the risk of incorrect outputs compared with general-purpose chatbots, while supporting natural interactions and the generation of answers, exercises, presentation slides, and lesson plans.

The proposed solution is built on Vietnamese high school Computer Science textbooks for grades 10 to 12 from the Canh Dieu and Ket Noi Tri Thuc series. The system includes one layer for user-request orchestration and another layer for content generation in tasks such as question answering, slide generation, and lesson-plan drafting. Textbook content is first preprocessed, segmented into structured units, and stored in a knowledge base for retrieval. When a request is received, the system first finds the most relevant textbook content and then uses a language model to generate the final response or instructional artifact. This design improves source grounding and makes output quality easier to control.

The main contribution of the thesis is an educational assistant developed in a research-oriented manner, where retrieval and content generation are evaluated separately. On a retrieval benchmark of 600 questions covering all 185 lessons, the system achieves Hit@1 of 0.8183, Hit@10 of 0.9833, and MRR@10 of 0.8856. On 246 generation-evaluation samples, it achieves Faithfulness of 0.9757 and Context Recall of 0.9593. These results show that the proposed approach is feasible for building a textbook-grounded learning assistant for Vietnamese high school Computer Science.
